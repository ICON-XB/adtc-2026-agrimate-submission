# Maize Farming Guide

## Soil Requirements
Maize grows best in well-drained, loamy soils with a pH between 5.5 and 7.0.
In Southern Africa, sandy soils may require additional organic matter or compost to retain moisture and nutrients.

## Water & Irrigation
Maize is susceptible to drought, especially during the silking stage.
- Early stage: Requires moderate watering. Overwatering can cause root rot.
- Silking to grain fill: This is the most critical period. Water stress here can reduce yields by 50%.
- Drought strategies: Use conservation agriculture techniques like mulching and minimum tillage to retain soil moisture.

## Common Pests
- **Fall Armyworm**: Identifiable by ragged leaves and sawdust-like frass. Control using early planting, intercropping with beans (Push-Pull strategy), and appropriate biopesticides.
- **Maize Stalk Borer**: Causes holes in leaves. Can be managed by destroying crop residue after harvest.

## Diseases
- **Maize Lethal Necrosis (MLN)**: Leaves turn yellow from the margins. Remove and destroy infected plants immediately.
- **Smut**: Causes large fungal galls. Remove galls before they burst.
