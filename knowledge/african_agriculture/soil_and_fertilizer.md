# Soil Management and Fertilizer Guidelines for African Agriculture

## Understanding Soil Nutrients
Plants require three primary macronutrients:
- **Nitrogen (N)**: Promotes leafy green growth. Deficiency causes pale yellow leaves starting from lower/older leaves.
- **Phosphorus (P)**: Promotes root development, flowering, and fruiting. Deficiency causes purple/reddish discoloration on leaves, poor root growth.
- **Potassium (K)**: Strengthens stems, disease resistance, and fruit quality. Deficiency causes brown scorching on leaf edges.

## Common Fertilizers and Application Rates

### Maize Fertilizer Program
- **Basal (at planting)**: Apply compound fertilizer (e.g., 2:3:2 or DAP) at 200-250 kg/ha in furrows 5cm below and beside the seed.
- **Top Dressing (4-6 weeks after planting)**: Apply Urea (46% N) or LAN (28% N) at 200 kg/ha as a side-dressing when maize is knee-high.
- **Important**: Never apply urea directly onto wet leaves; it causes leaf burn.

### Cassava Fertilizer Program
- **At Planting**: Apply NPK 15:15:15 at 400 kg/ha.
- **Key Need**: Cassava is a heavy potassium feeder. Apply muriate of potash (MOP) at 100-150 kg/ha for optimal root starch content.
- **Organic Alternative**: Well-composted manure at 5-10 tonnes/ha.

### Bean and Cowpea Fertilizer Program
- **At Planting**: Apply SSP (Single Superphosphate) or DAP at 100-150 kg/ha for phosphorus.
- **Note**: Beans and cowpeas are nitrogen-fixing legumes. Do NOT apply nitrogen fertilizer; it suppresses natural nitrogen fixation by rhizobium bacteria in root nodules.

### Tomato Fertilizer Program
- **Basal**: Apply 2:3:4 compound fertilizer at 800-1000 kg/ha in planting holes.
- **Top Dressing**: Apply LAN at 150 kg/ha every 3 weeks after transplanting until fruiting.
- **Calcium**: Apply agricultural lime or calcium nitrate to prevent blossom-end rot.

## Soil Preparation
- **Plowing**: Plow to a depth of 20-30cm to break compaction layers and incorporate crop residues.
- **Liming**: If soil pH is below 5.5, apply agricultural lime (dolomitic lime preferred) at 1-2 tonnes/ha at least 3 months before planting.
- **Composting**: Build compost heaps using crop residues, animal manure, kitchen waste, and wood ash. Turn every 2 weeks. Ready in 3-4 months.

## Crop Rotation Benefits
- Rotate cereals (maize, sorghum) with legumes (beans, cowpeas, groundnuts) to naturally replenish soil nitrogen.
- Rotate root crops (cassava, potatoes) with leafy crops to prevent buildup of soil-borne diseases.
- Never plant the same crop family in the same field for more than 2 consecutive seasons.

## Water Management
- **Drip Irrigation**: Most efficient method for vegetable gardens. Delivers water directly to root zones, reducing waste by 40-60%.
- **Mulching**: Apply 5-10cm of dry grass, straw, or crop residues around plants to retain soil moisture, suppress weeds, and regulate soil temperature.
- **Watering Frequency**: Most vegetables need 25-40mm of water per week. Water early morning or late afternoon to reduce evaporation losses.
