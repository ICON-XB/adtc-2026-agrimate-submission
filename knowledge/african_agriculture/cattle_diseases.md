# Cattle Diseases and Treatment Guidelines

## Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD)
- **Causative Agent**: Lumpy skin disease virus (Capripoxvirus family).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Firm, round skin nodules (2-5 cm diameter) appearing across the body, especially on the head, neck, limbs, udder, and genitals.
  - High fever (40-41°C), reduced milk yield, swollen lymph nodes, nasal and eye discharge.
  - Nodules may ulcerate and attract secondary infections (flies, screwworm).
  - Severe cases: emaciation, pneumonia, mastitis, infertility.
- **Transmission**: Spread by biting insects (mosquitoes, stable flies, ticks). NOT spread by direct contact.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Immediate Isolation**: Quarantine all infected cattle at least 100m from healthy stock.
  2. **Vector Control**: Apply pour-on insecticides (e.g., cypermethrin) to all cattle. Spray housing with residual insecticide.
  3. **Vaccination**: Vaccinate healthy cattle with live attenuated Neethling-strain LSD vaccine. Annual boosters are essential.
  4. **Supportive Care**: Administer anti-inflammatory drugs (flunixin meglumine). Apply wound spray (oxytetracycline aerosol) to open nodules. Broad-spectrum antibiotics for secondary bacterial infections.
  5. **Movement Control**: Do NOT move cattle during an outbreak. Report to local veterinary authority.

## East Coast Fever (ECF / Theileriosis)
- **Causative Agent**: *Theileria parva* (protozoan parasite), transmitted by the brown ear tick (*Rhipicephalus appendiculatus*).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - High fever, swollen lymph nodes (especially the parotid node near the ear).
  - Labored breathing, nasal discharge, loss of appetite.
  - Corneal opacity (cloudy eyes) in advanced cases.
  - Death can occur within 18-24 days without treatment.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Tick Control**: Dip or spray cattle with acaricides (amitraz, flumethrin) every 5-7 days during tick season.
  2. **Early Treatment**: Buparvaquone (Butalex) injection at 2.5 mg/kg body weight intramuscularly. Must be given EARLY for effectiveness.
  3. **Immunization**: Infection-and-treatment method (ITM) available in some East African countries.

## Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD)
- **Causative Agent**: Foot-and-mouth disease virus (Aphthovirus).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Blisters (vesicles) on the tongue, gums, lips, nostrils, feet (between hooves), and teats.
  - Excessive drooling/salivation, lameness, reluctance to move or eat.
  - High fever initially, followed by blister formation.
  - Blisters rupture leaving raw, painful ulcers.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Quarantine**: Strict isolation. FMD is HIGHLY contagious through air, saliva, milk, and contaminated equipment.
  2. **Notify Authorities**: FMD is a notifiable disease in most African countries. Report immediately.
  3. **Supportive Care**: Soft feed, clean water. Apply antiseptic (gentian violet) to mouth and foot lesions.
  4. **Vaccination**: Bi-annual vaccination with inactivated polyvalent FMD vaccine covering local serotypes.
  5. **Biosecurity**: Disinfect all footwear, vehicles, and equipment entering/leaving the farm with sodium carbonate solution.
