# Tomato Diseases and Pest Management

## Early Blight
- **Causative Agent**: *Alternaria solani* (Fungus).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Dark brown to black concentric ring spots (target-shaped) on older/lower leaves.
  - Yellowing around the spots. Leaves eventually dry up and fall off.
  - Can also infect stems (dark sunken lesions) and fruit (leathery dark spots near the stem end).
- **Action Plan**:
  1. Remove and destroy infected lower leaves immediately.
  2. Apply mancozeb or chlorothalonil fungicide every 7-10 days during wet weather.
  3. Stake and prune plants to improve air circulation.
  4. Mulch to prevent soil-borne spores from splashing onto leaves.
  5. Rotate tomatoes with non-solanaceous crops for at least 2 years.

## Late Blight
- **Causative Agent**: *Phytophthora infestans* (Oomycete).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Large, water-soaked, pale green to dark brown patches on leaves, often with white fuzzy mold on the underside.
  - Rapidly spreads in cool, wet weather. Entire plants can collapse within days.
  - Brown, firm rot on green fruit.
- **Action Plan**:
  1. Apply metalaxyl + mancozeb (Ridomil Gold) preventatively every 7 days during rainy/cool conditions.
  2. Destroy all infected plant material (do NOT compost).
  3. Avoid overhead irrigation; water at the base.
  4. Plant resistant varieties where available.

## Blossom End Rot
- **Cause**: Calcium deficiency in the fruit, usually triggered by inconsistent watering (not a disease).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Dark, sunken, leathery patch at the bottom (blossom end) of the fruit.
  - Affects green and ripening fruit.
- **Action Plan**:
  1. Water consistently; avoid alternating between drought and flooding.
  2. Apply calcium nitrate or agricultural gypsum to the soil.
  3. Mulch to maintain even soil moisture.
  4. Do NOT over-apply nitrogen fertilizer (it competes with calcium uptake).

## Tomato Fruit Worm (Helicoverpa armigera)
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Round entry holes bored into green or ripe fruit, with frass (caterpillar droppings) visible.
  - Caterpillars (green, brown, or pink, up to 4cm long) found inside the fruit.
- **Action Plan**:
  1. Handpick and destroy caterpillars and infested fruit.
  2. Apply Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) spray as a biological control.
  3. Use pheromone traps to monitor and reduce adult moth populations.
  4. Intercrop with marigolds or basil to repel moths.

## Whitefly and Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Whiteflies: Tiny white insects on the underside of leaves. Honeydew secretion causes sooty mold.
  - TYLCV: Severe upward curling of leaves, yellowing of leaf margins, stunted plant growth, drastically reduced fruit set.
- **Action Plan**:
  1. Use yellow sticky traps to monitor and reduce whitefly populations.
  2. Apply neem oil or insecticidal soap every 5-7 days.
  3. Remove and destroy TYLCV-infected plants immediately (there is no cure).
  4. Use reflective mulch to repel whiteflies.
  5. Plant TYLCV-resistant tomato varieties.
