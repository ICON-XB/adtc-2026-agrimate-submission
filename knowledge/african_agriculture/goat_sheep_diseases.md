# Goat and Sheep Skin Diseases and Treatment Guidelines

This document details diagnostic symptoms and treatments for goat skin diseases, specifically focusing on symptoms like hard, lumpy skin, scabs, and lesions.

## Sheep and Goat Pox (Capripoxvirus)
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Hard, round, lumpy nodules (papules) on the skin, especially on areas with little hair (under the tail, groin, ears, and face).
  - Nodules become necrotic, forming hard dry scabs that eventually fall off, leaving raw sores.
  - High fever, nasal discharge, swollen lymph nodes, and depression.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  - **No Specific Cure**: It is a viral disease; treatment focuses on supportive care and preventing secondary bacterial infections.
  - **Biosecurity**: Immediately isolate infected animals and restrict herd movement.
  - **Vaccination**: Vaccinate healthy animals using a live attenuated capripoxvirus vaccine.
  - **Secondary Treatment**: Apply antiseptic ointments to open skin sores. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g., oxytetracycline) to prevent secondary bacterial pneumonia.

## Mange (Sarcoptic or Psoroptic Mange Mites)
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Severe itching (pruritus), leading to intense scratching and biting of the skin.
  - Skin becomes thickened, hard, wrinkled, and covered in gray-yellow crusts or lumps.
  - Hair loss (alopecia), starting on the head/ears and spreading down the neck and body.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  - **Acaricide Dips/Sprays**: Treat the animal with amitraz or diazinon dips/sprays weekly for 2-3 weeks.
  - **Systemic Injectable**: Administer Ivermectin or Doramectin subcutaneously (1 ml per 50 kg body weight), repeated after 14 days to kill newly hatched mites.
  - **Environment Control**: Clean and spray the shelters/corrals with acaricides to kill mites living in the woodwork or bedding.

## Caseous Lymphadenitis (CL / Pseudotuberculosis)
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Hard, lumpy swelling or abscesses containing thick, greenish-yellow pus.
  - Located near major lymph nodes (under the jaw, neck, shoulder, or flank).
  - Abscesses eventually rupture and form thick scabs.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  - **Isolation**: Isolate affected goats immediately before the abscesses rupture.
  - **Abscess Management**: Flush ruptured abscesses with iodine or chlorhexidine. Burn or discard all pus-contaminated dressings securely.
  - **Antibiotic Therapy**: Long-term treatment with penicillin or rifampin (though CL is highly resistant inside encapsulated abscesses).
