# Maize Diseases and Treatment Guidelines

This document provides diagnostic criteria and treatment options for common maize diseases found in Africa, focusing on symptoms like yellowing leaves and dark/black spots.

## Northern Corn Leaf Blight (NCLB)
- **Causative Agent**: *Exserohilum turcicum* (Fungus).
- **Key Symptoms**: 
  - Long, cigar-shaped, grayish-green or tan lesions (1-6 inches long) on leaves.
  - As the disease progresses, these lesions turn yellow-brown with dark, dusty fungal spores (looks like dark/black spots or soot inside the lesions).
  - Leaves eventually wither and die under severe infection.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Resistant Varieties**: Plant certified NCLB-tolerant or resistant maize hybrids.
  2. **Crop Rotation**: Rotate maize with non-grass crops (e.g., legumes like cowpea, soybean, or groundnut) for at least one season.
  3. **Tillage**: Deep plow crop residues after harvest to bury fungal spores.
  4. **Chemical Control**: Apply triazole or strobilurin-based fungicides (e.g., azoxystrobin, tebuconazole) at the first sign of symptoms, particularly before silking.

## Southern Corn Leaf Blight (SCLB)
- **Causative Agent**: *Bipolaris maydis* (Fungus).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Small, oval, reddish-brown or tan spots (0.5 to 1 inch long) with yellow halos.
  - The spots can develop dark borders and black centers (spore clusters) on leaves, stalks, and husks.
  - Leaves yellow and dry out from the bottom of the plant upwards.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Sanitation**: Clean and burn infected crop debris to prevent overwintering.
  2. **Spacing**: Avoid high-density planting to improve airflow and reduce humidity in the crop canopy.
  3. **Fungicides**: Apply mancozeb or propiconazole if weather is warm and humid and symptoms appear early in the season.

## Grey Leaf Spot (GLS)
- **Causative Agent**: *Cercospora zeae-maydis* (Fungus).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Rectangular, tan or gray lesions restricted by leaf veins.
  - Starts as small yellow spots with dark centers, which expand into long blocky strips.
  - Severely blighted leaves turn yellow, dry out, and die.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. Rotate with non-host crops.
  2. Use zero-tillage with caution: if GLS is present, residue management (shredding or burying) is vital.
  3. Apply systemic fungicides pre-tassel if disease pressure is high.
