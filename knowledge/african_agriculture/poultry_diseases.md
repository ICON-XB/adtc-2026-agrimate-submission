# Poultry Diseases and Treatment Guidelines

## Newcastle Disease (ND)
- **Causative Agent**: Newcastle disease virus (Paramyxovirus).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - **Respiratory**: Gasping, coughing, sneezing, rattling sounds when breathing.
  - **Nervous**: Twisted neck (torticollis), circling, paralysis of wings and legs, tremors.
  - **Digestive**: Greenish-yellow watery diarrhea, drop in egg production, soft-shelled or misshapen eggs.
  - High mortality (up to 100% in unvaccinated flocks).
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **No Cure**: Newcastle disease has NO effective treatment once infected.
  2. **Vaccination**: Use thermostable I-2 vaccine (heat-stable, ideal for rural Africa) via eye drop every 3-4 months. La Sota strain vaccine for commercial flocks.
  3. **Biosecurity**: Isolate sick birds immediately. Do not introduce new birds without quarantine. Disinfect housing, feeders, and waterers with virkon or bleach.
  4. **Disposal**: Dead birds must be burned or deeply buried to prevent spread.

## Fowl Pox
- **Causative Agent**: Avipoxvirus.
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - **Dry Form**: Raised, wart-like nodules/scabs on the comb, wattles, eyelids, and unfeathered skin areas.
  - **Wet Form**: Yellow, cheesy plaques inside the mouth, throat, and trachea causing difficulty breathing and swallowing.
  - Drop in egg production, weight loss, secondary infections.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Supportive Care**: Apply iodine or gentian violet to dry pox lesions. Remove wet pox plaques gently and apply antiseptic.
  2. **Antibiotics**: Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics in water to control secondary bacterial infections.
  3. **Vaccination**: Wing-web vaccination of chicks at 6-8 weeks of age with live fowl pox vaccine.

## Coccidiosis
- **Causative Agent**: *Eimeria* species (protozoan parasites).
- **Key Symptoms**:
  - Bloody or dark reddish-brown droppings (the hallmark sign).
  - Huddling, ruffled feathers, pale comb, reduced feed intake.
  - Sudden deaths in young chicks (3-6 weeks old).
  - Weight loss and poor growth in surviving birds.
- **Action Plan & Treatments**:
  1. **Anticoccidial Drugs**: Administer amprolium (Corid) in drinking water for 5-7 days. Toltrazuril as a single oral dose.
  2. **Litter Management**: Keep litter dry. Wet litter promotes oocyst sporulation.
  3. **Prevention**: Use coccidiostat-medicated starter feed for chicks. Vaccination with live oocyst vaccine is available for commercial operations.

## Reasons Chickens Stop Laying Eggs
- **Insufficient Light**: Hens need 14-16 hours of light per day. Supplement with artificial lighting during short days.
- **Poor Nutrition**: Lack of calcium (provide oyster shell grit), insufficient protein, or inadequate feed quantity.
- **Stress**: Overcrowding, predator threats, sudden changes in housing, extreme heat or cold.
- **Molting**: Natural feather replacement cycle. Egg production stops during molt and resumes after 8-12 weeks.
- **Age**: Production declines after 18-24 months. Consider flock replacement.
- **Disease**: Newcastle disease, infectious bronchitis, egg drop syndrome. Vaccinate preventively.
- **Parasites**: Heavy worm or mite infestations reduce production. Deworm regularly and treat for red mites.
