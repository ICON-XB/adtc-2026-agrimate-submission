# Drought Stress and Water Management for African Crops

## Signs of Drought Stress in Maize
- **Early Stage**: Leaf rolling/curling during the hottest part of the day. Leaves unfurl again in the evening.
- **Moderate Stage**: Leaves remain rolled throughout the day. Gray-green discoloration. Delayed silking, causing poor pollination.
- **Severe Stage**: Leaves turn brown and fire from the bottom up. Tassels emerge before silks (asynchronous flowering). Small, partially filled ears. Barren stalks.
- **Critical Period**: The 2 weeks before and after silking is the most sensitive period. Drought during this window can reduce yields by 50-100%.

## Drought Mitigation Strategies
1. **Conservation Agriculture**: Minimum tillage, permanent soil cover (mulch), and crop rotation. Reduces evaporation by 30-50%.
2. **Tied Ridges**: Create small dams between ridges to trap rainfall and prevent runoff. Effective in semi-arid zones.
3. **Rainwater Harvesting**: Collect roof runoff into tanks or underground cisterns. A 100m² roof can collect 50,000 liters per year with 500mm rainfall.
4. **Drought-Tolerant Varieties**: Plant DTMA (Drought Tolerant Maize for Africa) varieties developed by CIMMYT/IITA. These maintain 20-30% higher yields under water stress.
5. **Early Planting**: Plant with the first effective rains to maximize the growing season. Delays of 2 weeks can reduce yields by 10-20%.
6. **Mulching**: Apply 5-10cm of dry grass, straw, or crop residues to reduce soil evaporation.
7. **Intercropping**: Plant maize with drought-tolerant legumes (cowpeas, pigeon pea) to create ground cover and reduce evaporation.

## Irrigation Methods Ranked by Efficiency
| Method | Efficiency | Best For |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| Drip irrigation | 85-95% | Vegetables, fruit trees |
| Sprinkler | 65-80% | Field crops, pastures |
| Furrow | 45-65% | Row crops (maize, sugarcane) |
| Flood/Basin | 30-50% | Rice paddies only |

## When to Irrigate
- **Finger Test**: Push finger 5cm into soil. If dry, irrigate.
- **Early Morning**: Best time to irrigate (4-8 AM). Minimizes evaporation and disease risk.
- **Avoid Night Watering**: Prolonged leaf wetness promotes fungal diseases.
