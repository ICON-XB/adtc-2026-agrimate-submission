# Pan-African Agricultural Context

## West Africa
- **Key Crops**: Cocoa, yams, cassava, palm oil, millet, and sorghum.
- **Challenges**: Desertification in the Sahel region, irregular rainfall, and pests like the cocoa pod borer.
- **Practices**: Agroforestry and intercropping are widely used to maintain soil fertility and provide shade for cash crops.

## East Africa
- **Key Crops**: Coffee, tea, maize, beans, and horticultural products (flowers, vegetables).
- **Challenges**: Frequent droughts, Rift Valley fever in livestock, and the Desert Locust plagues.
- **Practices**: Terracing in hilly regions (like Rwanda and Kenya) to prevent soil erosion; widespread use of mobile money (e.g., M-Pesa) for agricultural financing.

## North Africa
- **Key Crops**: Wheat, barley, olives, citrus fruits, and dates.
- **Challenges**: Extreme water scarcity, soil salinization, and high temperatures.
- **Practices**: Extensive irrigation systems, drought-resistant crop varieties, and greenhouse agriculture.

## Central Africa
- **Key Crops**: Cassava, plantains, rubber, timber, and coffee.
- **Challenges**: Dense rainforest environments make large-scale farming difficult; poor infrastructure limits market access.
- **Practices**: Slash-and-burn agriculture (shifting cultivation) is common, though efforts are shifting towards sustainable agro-ecology.

## Southern Africa
- **Key Crops**: Maize, wheat, sugarcane, grapes (wine), and citrus.
- **Challenges**: El Niño-induced droughts, soil acidity, and livestock diseases (e.g., Foot-and-Mouth Disease).
- **Practices**: Conservation agriculture (minimum tillage) and advanced commercial irrigation in large-scale farms.
